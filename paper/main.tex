\title{Evaluation-Target Mismatch in Longitudinal Motor-Imagery BCI:\
Leave-One-Session-Out and Expanding-Window Validation}

\author{\uppercase{Yiming Shen}\authorrefmark{1}}

Boston, MA 02125 USA (e-mail: yiming.shen001@umb.edu)}
\markboth

{Shen: Evaluation-Target Mismatch in Longitudinal Motor-Imagery BCI}
\corresp{Corresponding author: Yiming Shen (e-mail: yiming.shen001@umb.edu).}

\begin{abstract}
Cross-session electroencephalography studies often use leave-one-session-out
(LOSO) evaluation, which trains on every recorded session except the target.
For an early target, this training set contains sessions recorded later in the
participant's trajectory. LOSO therefore measures retrospective compatibility
with a completed trajectory, whereas a prospective update can use only the
labeled history available at the target time. We quantified the resulting
evaluation-target mismatch by pairing LOSO with expanding-window validation at
identical target sessions in six longitudinal motor-imagery datasets. The audit
covered 138 participants and two established decoder families: common spatial
patterns with linear discriminant analysis and tangent-space features with
logistic regression. Across all prospective origins, LOSO reported an average
area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) that was 0.030 higher.
The mean difference was positive under each aggregation
rule and for both decoders, with substantial variation across recording designs.
The higher-scoring decoder differed between protocols for some participants,
primarily where the two decoders had similar scores. A donor-session-count-matched
reference partitioned the protocol difference into two descriptive contrasts.
Regrouping within-day blocks into recording days preserved a positive conditional
difference. Longitudinal BCI evaluation should define the
deployment decision and its available information before choosing the split.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
\PARstart{L}{ongitudinal} brain--computer interfaces (BCIs) are evaluated at a
sequence of calibration decisions. Before a new session, a decoder can use
previously labeled recordings; after the session, newly acquired labels may
support another update. An evaluation protocol represents the intended use only
when its training information matches what is available at that decision point.

Leave-one-session-out (LOSO) validation withholds one target session and trains
on every other recorded session. It is a natural measure of how well a session
fits the participant's completed recording trajectory and is available in
reproducible BCI benchmarking frameworks \cite{jayaram2018moabb,chevallier2024benchmark}.
At an early target, however, LOSO also uses later sessions. An expanding-window
design trains on sessions observed before the target. The two protocols test the
same trials but estimate different operational quantities.

This distinction matters because cross-session research increasingly targets
adaptation, transfer, and continual updating. A protocol that mixes later
recordings into an earlier training set can change both the reported performance
level and the apparent ordering of candidate decoders. Quantifying this difference
requires paired evaluation at identical test sessions, explicit averaging weights,
and a separation between the observed trajectory and the information available
before each target.

Building on rolling-origin evaluation and pipeline-level assessment, this study
quantifies the difference between retrospective and chronological training
information sets at identical BCI test sessions. First, it measures the paired
protocol difference across six datasets, 138 participants, and two decoder
families. Second, it characterizes variation across participants and recording
designs, observed decoder rankings, and a donor-session-count-matched reference.
Third, it provides a reporting specification linking each evaluation to its
prediction event, available information, temporal unit, and averaging population.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
Open BCI benchmarks standardize datasets, pipelines, and evaluation interfaces,
making cross-study comparison increasingly reproducible
\cite{jayaram2018moabb,chevallier2024benchmark}. Protocol validity remains a
separate requirement. Borra \emph{et al.} combine pipeline-wide hyperparameter
optimization with repeated random initialization in a within-participant LOSO protocol
\cite{borra2025trustworthy}. Brookshire \emph{et al.} examine leakage in
translational EEG studies \cite{brookshire2024leakage}. The present audit addresses
the time at which donor recordings become available while fixing decoder
configurations across protocols.

Cross-session motor-imagery methods address nonstationarity through session
selection, domain adaptation, and continual updating. Recent examples include
relevant-session transfer \cite{sung2024sessiontransfer}, synchronized
self-training \cite{chen2025synchronized}, nonstationary attention with
critic-free adaptation \cite{guo2025crosssession}, and longitudinal fine-tuning
\cite{wimpff2025continual}. These methods differ in architecture and adaptation
mechanism, but all require an explicit definition of which sessions and labels
exist when a prediction is evaluated.

Temporal prediction research supplies that definition. Rolling-origin evaluation
places the model at the historical point from which a prediction would have been
made \cite{tashman2000outofsample}; leave-future-out validation and assessment
under temporal distribution shift apply the same principle
\cite{burkner2020leavefuture,han2024temporal}. The present study transfers this
principle within longitudinal BCI evaluation and measures the consequence of replacing
a completed-trajectory training set with the history actually available at each
target.

\section{Evaluation Targets and Estimands}
\label{sec:estimands}
\subsection{One Target, Two Information Sets}
For participant $i$ with ordered sessions $0,\ldots,T_i-1$, the donor sets at
target session $t$ are
\begin{align}
S_{\LOSO}(t) &= \{0,\ldots,T_i-1\}\setminus\{t\},\\
S_{\EXP}(t) &= \{0,\ldots,t-1\}.
\end{align}
Both protocols reserve every trial from session $t$ for testing. Performance is
measured by the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC).
For decoder $m$, the paired contrast is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{i,t,m}=\AUC^{\LOSO}_{i,t,m}-\AUC^{\EXP}_{i,t,m}.
\end{equation}
Evaluation begins at $t=1$, when at least one prior session exists. At the final
target, the two donor sets coincide, so $\Delta_{i,T_i-1,m}=0$. Figure~\ref{fig:logic}
shows the information-set comparison and the two analysis populations.

\subsection{Primary and Supporting Estimands}
Let $N=138$ and let $m$ index the two fixed decoder configurations, CSP+LDA and
TS+LR. The primary estimand covers every prospective origin in the observed trajectories:
\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{all}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i
\frac{1}{2(T_i-1)}\sum_m\sum_{t=1}^{T_i-1}\Delta_{i,t,m}.
\end{equation}
The supporting conditional estimand describes targets at which LOSO has at least
one later donor:
\begin{equation}
\theta_{\mathrm{future}>0}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_i
\frac{1}{2(T_i-2)}\sum_m\sum_{t=1}^{T_i-2}\Delta_{i,t,m}.
\end{equation}
Both estimands first average targets and the two decoders within participant and
then give participants equal weight. Target-weighted summaries instead give each
participant--target pair equal weight after averaging decoders. Dataset-weighted
summaries average the six dataset means equally.
Writing $\bar\Delta_i$ for the within-participant average over targets and both
decoders, the final structural zero gives the identity
\begin{equation}
\bar\Delta_{i,\mathrm{all}}
=\frac{T_i-2}{T_i-1}\bar\Delta_{i,\mathrm{future}>0}.
\label{eq:zero-scaling}
\end{equation}
Thus, all-origin means summarize the complete observed sequence of update
decisions, while conditional means summarize decisions with at least one later
donor. The scaling factor varies with trajectory length.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}
\subsection{Datasets and Time Units}
The analysis uses the fixed collection of six datasets in Table~\ref{tab:data}.
Each provides binary motor-imagery labels, at least three source-defined ordered
units per participant, chronological unit identifiers, and complete evaluation
coverage for both classical decoders. The inclusion manifest records the
participants and targets represented in the result files. This curated collection
defines the empirical scope of the audit, comprising 138 participants.

Zhou2016 and Zhou2020 are distinct datasets \cite{zhou2016trial,zhou2021relative}.
Ma2020 records right-hand and right-elbow imagery \cite{ma2020samejoint}; the
remaining datasets use left- and right-hand imagery. The source-defined unit is a
session or recording day except in the primary Ma2020 analysis, which uses the 15
released block identifiers. A sensitivity analysis regroups those blocks into
their three recording days. Session-index lag is interpreted within dataset
because a one-unit step does not represent a common elapsed time across studies.

Every recording was band-pass filtered from 8 to 35 Hz, resampled to 128 Hz, and
epoched over $[0,3)$ s relative to the task cue, giving 384 samples per trial.
Ma2020 CNT files were loaded after removing horizontal and vertical
electrooculography channels, the mastoid reference, and optional electromyography
channels, leaving 62 scalp EEG channels.
Preprocessing did not use participant-wide or cross-session normalization, and
its parameters did not depend on target-session labels or performance.

\subsection{Decoders and Information Isolation}
The paired audit used two established decoder families with different feature
geometries and complete coverage across all six datasets. Common spatial patterns
with linear discriminant analysis (CSP+LDA) represent a supervised spatial-filter
pipeline; tangent-space features with logistic regression (TS+LR) represent
covariance geometry in a tangent space. Their deterministic, fixed configurations keep the comparison
centered on evaluation protocol rather than decoder search.

CSP+LDA used $\min(8,p)$ common spatial pattern (CSP) components for $p$ channels,
reduced to the next lower even number when required. CSP used Ledoit--Wolf
regularization, and linear discriminant analysis used the \texttt{lsqr} solver
with automatic shrinkage \cite{blankertz2008csp}. TS+LR estimated a Ledoit--Wolf
covariance for each trial, learned the affine-invariant tangent-space reference
from the donor covariances, and fitted $L_2$ logistic regression with $C=1$ and
the \texttt{lbfgs} solver \cite{barachant2012riemann}. All hyperparameters were
held fixed across protocols.

Each participant, target, protocol, and decoder received a fresh fit. CSP filters,
LDA parameters, the tangent-space reference, and logistic-regression parameters
were estimated only from donor trials. The target session contributed final
scores only. The inclusion audit verified complete participant--target fits,
unique result keys, and AUC values in the valid range. AUC was the primary endpoint;
the Ma2020 day-level sensitivity also reported balanced accuracy.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
The primary analysis used participant-level all-origin means, two-sided one-sample
$t$ intervals, and Wilcoxon signed-rank sensitivity tests. The same procedures
were applied to the conditional estimand. Target-weighted intervals used 5,000
participant-cluster bootstrap resamples. Dataset means were summarized with
participant-level intervals and equal-dataset weighting.

A random-effects synthesis used each dataset's participant-level mean and sampling
variance. Between-dataset variance was estimated by restricted maximum likelihood.
The summary interval used a $t$ reference across the six datasets, and the
prediction interval incorporated mean-estimation uncertainty and between-dataset
variance. Heterogeneity was summarized by $I^2$. Exact interval formulas and a
residual-scale sensitivity analysis are given in Appendix~\ref{app:uncertainty}.
The intervals describe estimation uncertainty without multiplicity adjustment;
secondary contrasts and sensitivity checks are interpreted descriptively.

Protocol sensitivity was evaluated from the participant distribution of $\Delta$
and the higher-scoring decoder by participant mean AUC under each protocol.
We recorded the direction of each observed ranking change and its persistence
when both within-protocol decoder differences exceeded 0.005, 0.01, or 0.02 AUC.
These thresholds describe proximity to a tie. Two additional AUC thresholds,
0.70 and 0.75, were retained as illustrative sensitivity checks in the appendix;
they were not treated as clinical cutoffs.

\subsection{Common-Support and Robustness Analyses}
A uniform donor-session-count-matched reference sampled $t$ sessions from the
LOSO-eligible donor pool and averaged five draws from a fixed random seed.
The main decomposition uses all conditional targets. A second decomposition uses
strict-future common support, $T_i-1-t\geq t$, to align with an additional arm
trained exclusively on later sessions. On each support, the contrast is
partitioned as
\begin{align}
\AUC_{\LOSO}-\AUC_{\EXP}={}&
(\AUC_{\LOSO}-\AUC_{\mathrm{matched}})\nonumber\\
&+(\AUC_{\mathrm{matched}}-\AUC_{\EXP}).
\end{align}
The first term compares the full donor pool with the matched reference; the second
compares that reference with the $t$ prior sessions. Matching session count leaves
trial count and session characteristics dependent on the sampled donors. The
components form an additive description under this specific reference and support.
Participant intervals condition on the realized draws. Monte Carlo diagnostics
use support-specific weights and a covariance-free bound based on estimated
marginal variances (Appendix~\ref{app:uncertainty}).

Two additional operational policies trained on the immediately previous session
or the first session only. The target-position analysis related the conditional
contrast to the number of later sessions available to LOSO within each dataset.
An adjacent-session drift proxy used the affine-invariant Riemannian distance
between unconditional mean trial covariance matrices at $t-1$ and $t$, standardized
within dataset. Its target-level regression adjusted for target index, decoder,
and dataset, with participant-clustered standard errors. Finally, the Ma2020
day-level analysis concatenated blocks 1--5, 6--10, and 11--15, retained all trials,
and refitted both decoders.

\subsection{Decomposition Under a Uniform Donor-Session Reference}
On all 1,001 conditional participant--target pairs, the total protocol difference
was \UniformTotalMean{} AUC. The full-pool minus matched-reference component was
\UniformCountMean{}, and the matched-reference minus prior-history component was
\UniformCompositionMean{}. Both comparisons use the same targets and participant
weights as the conditional estimand. The reference matches the number of donor
sessions; variation in their trial counts remains within the sampled composition.

Restricting all arms to the 560 early targets with sufficient strictly future
donors gave a total difference of \StrictTotalMean{} AUC, partitioned into
\StrictCountMean{} and \StrictCompositionMean{}. Training exclusively on the
matched number of future sessions instead of prior sessions gave a difference of
\StrictFutureMean{}. Table~\ref{tab:common-support} reports each support separately.
The covariance-free plug-in standard-error bounds for the sampled reference were
\CondMatchedTriangleSE{} and \StrictMatchedTriangleSE{} AUC on these two supports.
They use estimated marginal variances; the joint Monte Carlo standard error is
unidentified from the retained summaries. Appendix~\ref{app:uncertainty} specifies
the diagnostic separately from participant uncertainty.

\subsection{Temporal-Unit and Drift Analyses}
For Ma2020, the all-origin difference was \MaBlockAll{} AUC when the released
blocks were treated as ordered units and \MaDayAll{} after the same trials were
grouped into three recording days. The conditional differences were
\MaBlockCond{} and \MaDayCond{} AUC, respectively; the day-level difference was
also positive for balanced accuracy. Equation~\eqref{eq:zero-scaling} gives an
all-origin scaling factor of $13/14$ for blocks and $1/2$ for days. Thus, the
all-origin comparison combines a change in the temporal unit with a change in
the weight of the final structural zero. The conditional comparison retains
a positive separation under both time units.
Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity} also reports the immediately-previous-session and
first-session policy contrasts.

The target-position curves differed by dataset and were largest at early positions
in several longer trajectories. The adjusted coefficient for the adjacent
unconditional covariance-distance proxy was near zero. These analyses are shown
with their support and influence checks in Appendix~\ref{app:robust}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\subsection{The Split Defines the Evaluation Target}
The main result quantifies how the training information set changes the measured
performance at an identical target. LOSO measures decoding after the completed
participant trajectory is available.
Expanding-window validation measures decoding from the labeled history available
before each target. The same participant, target trials, preprocessing, and decoder
were paired throughout; the designed difference was the training information set.

The primary all-origin estimate answers the longitudinal decision-sequence question
over the recorded observation period. The conditional estimate summarizes targets
where LOSO has later donors. Equation~\eqref{eq:zero-scaling} connects these
quantities and shows how the observed endpoint and trajectory length affect
all-origin averages. Reporting both preserves the distinction between the full
decision sequence and the subset with different training information.

\subsection{Consequences for Benchmarking and Adaptation}
The protocol changed the pooled performance level and the observed higher-scoring
decoder for a subset of participants. Most all-origin ranking changes involved
decoder scores within 0.01 AUC under at least one protocol. These results describe
the sensitivity of observed rankings to the split; a prospective selection policy
would additionally define how a decoder is chosen from available history and
evaluate that choice on subsequent sessions. Reporting the decoder margin alongside
the winner count separates near ties from larger ranking changes.

Under the uniform donor-session reference, the full-pool versus matched-reference
component was larger than the matched-reference versus prior-history component.
The latter holds session count fixed while changing donor identity, trial count,
and session characteristics. This reference-dependent description identifies which
comparisons differ in score. Session-selection and adaptation studies can use the
same construction to state precisely which donor pool each reported score represents.

Participant- and target-weighted summaries were closely aligned, whereas the
random-effects analysis showed substantial cross-dataset heterogeneity. The result
shows a positive mean in each of the six observed datasets and a design-specific
magnitude. The random-effects prediction interval includes both positive and
negative values. Dataset-level effects and the prediction interval therefore
remain part of the interpretation of the pooled estimate.

\subsection{Reporting Procedure and Empirical Scope}
A longitudinal evaluation can be specified by the five reporting fields in
Table~\ref{tab:reporting}. A completed-trajectory question maps to LOSO;
chronological offline retraining maps to an expanding window. When both questions
matter, both estimates can be reported and named by their information sets.

The empirical estimates describe binary motor imagery, two fixed classical decoder
configurations, and the six recording designs in Table~\ref{tab:data}. The evaluated
update policy refits from fully labeled prior sessions and measures discrimination
by AUC on fixed recorded trajectories. Neural decoders, multiclass outcomes,
delayed labels, and closed-loop adaptation define additional empirical settings.
Feedback and user learning, as studied in closed-loop BCI
\cite{wang2026jointlearning}, add interactions between the decoder and the recorded
trajectory. Their evaluation requires specifying the corresponding update events
and information availability. Alternative donor-sampling references and outcome
metrics likewise define additional comparisons.

\section{Conclusion}
LOSO and expanding-window validation estimate different longitudinal BCI targets.
Across six motor-imagery datasets, LOSO reported an average all-origin AUC 0.030
higher, with variation across recording designs. Observed participant-level decoder
rankings changed mainly where the scores were close. A uniform donor-session-count
reference provided an additive description of the conditional difference on
explicit support. Longitudinal benchmarks should define the evaluation decision,
match its training information, and report its temporal unit and averaging weights.

\section{Interval Definitions and Donor-Sampling Uncertainty}
\label{app:uncertainty}
\subsection{Dataset-Level Synthesis}
Let $y_d$ and $v_d$ be the participant mean and its estimated sampling variance
for dataset $d$, and let $k=6$. With restricted-maximum-likelihood estimate
$\widehat\tau^2$, the weights and pooled mean are
\begin{equation}
w_d=(v_d+\widehat\tau^2)^{-1},\qquad
\widehat\mu=\frac{\sum_d w_d y_d}{\sum_d w_d}.
\end{equation}
The reported interval is $\widehat\mu\pm
t_{k-1,0.975}(\sum_d w_d)^{-1/2}$; the prediction interval is
$\widehat\mu\pm t_{k-2,0.975}
\sqrt{\widehat\tau^2+(\sum_d w_d)^{-1}}$.
As a residual-scale sensitivity analysis, define
$q=\sum_d w_d(y_d-\widehat\mu)^2/(k-1)$ and replace the mean standard error
by $\sqrt{q/\sum_d w_d}$. This gives intervals of
[\AllMetaHKLo{}, \AllMetaHKHi{}] for all origins and
[\CondMetaHKLo{}, \CondMetaHKHi{}] for conditional targets.

\subsection{Five-Draw Uniform Reference}
The cached per-row standard deviation $s_{r,0}$ uses divisor $B=5$.
The sample-standard-deviation correction gives a marginal standard error of
$e_r=s_{r,0}/\sqrt{B-1}$ for the sampled row mean. Rows share random draws
across decoders; the cached summaries retain marginal variation but not the
cross-row covariances. For nonnegative participant-averaging weights $a_r$
summing to one, we report
\begin{equation}
U=\sum_r a_r e_r.
\end{equation}
The triangle inequality bounds the aggregate standard error by $U$ under the
estimated marginal variances, allowing arbitrary cross-row covariance.
This is a plug-in bound rather than a statistical confidence upper limit.
The corresponding values are \CondMatchedTriangleSE{} AUC on all conditional
targets and \StrictMatchedTriangleSE{} on strict-future support. The result files
also report the median, 95th percentile, and maximum marginal row-level standard
error. Participant confidence intervals for the partition condition on
the realized donor draws; donor-sampling uncertainty is reported separately.
The unsampled LOSO--expanding contrast is unchanged by the reference draws.
